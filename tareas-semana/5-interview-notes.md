## Questions and Answers (summary)

**Q: How long have you worked in this profession?**
A: [Check your recording: the transcript says "60 years," which is probably a transcription error]

**Q: How did you decide to dedicate yourself to this field?**
A: Book editor in Korea for 10 years. Saw reading and writing as communication (sending and receiving messages). Went to grad school (four different schools) and became a communication teacher.

**Q: What do you like most about your work?**
A: Meeting people.

**Q: What is the most complex thing you have had to write?**
A: Research papers. Many sections (abstract, intro, method, analysis, discussion, conclusion, references). Each one reflects years of work, not just a summary.

**Q: Did anyone teach you?**
A: Professors in grad school. It took her 10 years to learn.

**Q: What advice would you have liked when you started?**
A: Clear templates to follow. Now she gives her students templates: fill in the blanks, start easier, it doesn't have to be perfect.

**Q: What is your writing process?**
A: Outline first with bullet points (intro 1-2-3, body 1-2-3). Main points first, details later. If the structure doesn't work, she starts over without wasting much time.

**Q: What do you do with writer's block?**
A: New experiences: a new place, new restaurant, new food (maybe Cuban!). Needs "full-body processing," not just reading.

**Q: How do you get feedback?**
A: Paid AI version. Asks for comments and suggestions, NOT rewrites. Friends and husband are too busy.

**Q: A piece of writing that is personal to you?**
A: Her papers are "like my children." Most recent one took 10 years: Korean small-store owners with limited English building relationships with US customers. About to be published.

**Q: One that didn't turn out well?**
A: Paper based on interviews with North Koreans. Took 6 years, published in a weaker journal. Started too early in grad school, poor interview design.

**Q: What did you learn from it?**
A: Preparing and designing interviews is as important as writing. Have everything ready before you start writing.

**Q: How do you handle criticism?**
A: Presents at conferences (next: Atlanta, March 2027). Comments help; criticism hurts. Key: "Don't take it personally."

**Q: Did your writing style change?**
A: Yes. Editor = audience-centered, easy to follow. Academic = author-centered, more intellectual, for a small group.

**Q: What do you think about AI?**
A: Very useful for comments and suggestions.

**Q: One piece of advice for students?**
A: Practice. Writing is like speaking. Reading is different from writing, and short texts (Instagram) are different from long writing.

**Q: What resource do you recommend?**
A: Google Scholar: "standing on the shoulders of giants."
