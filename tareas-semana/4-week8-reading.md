## Part 1: Chapter 12 Exercise — Ask People Who Know You to Name Your Strengths (Section 12.2)

Source: OpenStax, *College Success*, Section 12.2 "Your Map to Success: The Career Planning Cycle," under "Learn About Yourself" / "Enlist Others"
https://openstax.org/books/college-success/pages/12-2-your-map-to-success-the-career-planning-cycle

**Instructions (verbatim from the text):**

> Choose one of the suggestions from the list, above, and follow through on it. Keep a log or journal of your experience with the activity and note how this might help you think about your future after college.

**Suggestion I chose (verbatim from the "Enlist Others" list):**

> Ask people who know you to tell you what they think your strengths are. This information can come from friends, classmates, professors, advisors, family members, coaches, mentors, and others. What kinds of things have they observed you doing well? What personal qualities do you have that they value? You are not asking them to tell you what career you should be in; rather, you are looking to learn more about yourself.

### My log

**Who I asked, and when**

- **Sept. 29, my mom (over the phone).** I asked what she thinks I am good at. She said I am patient when I explain something, and that I finish projects even when I get bored with them. She mentioned that when I was in high school I spent a whole weekend fixing the layout of the family's church newsletter website, and that I did not even ask for credit for it.
- **Oct. 1, my roommate, Dario.** He said I am the person people come to when a computer breaks, and that I figured out why his laptop kept freezing by reading the error logs instead of just restarting it. He said he values that I explain things without making him feel dumb.
- **Oct. 2, my UNIV 1101 instructor, during office hours.** I asked her what she noticed in our class discussions. She said I ask questions that connect the reading to things outside class, and that my Week 6 reflection showed I can organize my thoughts clearly on paper. She suggested I keep that habit.
- **Oct. 4, a friend from my high school art club.** She said I have a good eye for layout and color, and that people trust my feedback on their designs because I tell them what is wrong and why.

**What people have seen me do well (my summary)**

- Working through problems step by step instead of guessing
- Building things that look clean and work, including web pages and small 3D models
- Explaining technical things in plain language

**Personal qualities people value in me**

- Patience and persistence
- Honesty when giving feedback
- Being dependable once I commit to a project

**What surprised me**

I expected people to name art or design right away because that is how I usually describe myself. Instead, most of what they named was problem solving and finishing things. That made me think computer science may fit the way I already work, but my friend's comment about layout also shows that design is part of how I think. It makes the CS versus design choice feel less like picking one identity and more like picking which skills I want to build first.

**How this might help me after college**

Hearing these answers from four different people helped me see a pattern I did not notice before. Next I plan to use this list when I write my resume and when I visit Career Services, so I can describe myself with examples instead of just saying I am "good with computers." I also want to ask two more people, a professor in the computing department and my cousin who works as a web developer, to see whether they name the same strengths.

The paragraph below is written for the Career Services assignment. Pages I read are listed in the notes file.

Of everything on the Career Services site, Handshake seems the most useful to me right now. I am still deciding between computer science and graphic or web design, and the websites I build and the 3D models I make could fit either path, so I want to see real internships in both fields before I commit to one. The Career Planning page describes Handshake as the place to find internships and part-time or full-time jobs. This semester, I plan to create my Handshake profile, upload a one-page resume, and attend the "Ace the Interview" workshop on the events calendar before I apply anywhere. Next spring, I will look for an on-campus or part-time job in Handshake and book an appointment through the Career Services Bookings link so an advisor can review my resume and a portfolio of my web projects and 3D models. During my sophomore year, I will apply for summer internships through Handshake, go to the fall career and internship fair, and take the YouScience career assessment the office offers to see whether my interests point more toward programming or design. My goal is to finish college with at least one internship and a portfolio I can show an employer, not just a diploma.
